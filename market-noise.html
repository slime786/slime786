MARKETS / THINKING

Market noise versus business progress.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · EDITORIAL DRAFT

A share price can change every second. The underlying business does not. That difference sounds obvious, but it is easy to forget when markets make every movement feel important.

I am interested in separating information about the business from information about the market’s mood. A price move can be worth investigating, but it does not automatically tell me whether customers are happier, a product is stronger, margins are improving or a competitive advantage is becoming more durable.

This does not mean price is irrelevant. Valuation matters, expectations matter and markets can reveal information quickly. I simply find it useful to ask what has actually changed before treating movement as meaning.

The same discipline applies to headlines. Some developments alter a company’s long-term economics. Others dominate attention for a day and then disappear.

I want my research process to become better at telling those two categories apart. That is one reason I built the Market Radar: not to predict every move, but to create a place for questions before conclusions.
