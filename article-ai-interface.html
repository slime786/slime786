AI is becoming an interface, not just a feature.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · 4 MIN READ

The most interesting part of the AI shift is not simply that models can generate text, images or code. It is that intelligence is moving closer to the interface itself.

For years, software asked people to learn menus, workflows and commands. AI creates the possibility that the interface can increasingly learn the person instead.

That changes what a useful product can feel like. Search can become synthesis. A blank document can become a collaborator. Complicated software can expose its capabilities through conversation rather than layers of controls.

The difficult part is trust. A system that sounds confident but behaves unpredictably creates a different design problem from conventional software. Good AI products will need to communicate uncertainty, preserve user control and make it easy to verify important outcomes.

The question I keep coming back to is simple: when intelligence becomes abundant, which products become dramatically easier to use — and which new products become possible at all?
