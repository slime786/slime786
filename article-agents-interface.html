Agents and the next interface shift.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · EDITORIAL DRAFT

Most software waits. A person opens an application, chooses a tool and tells it exactly what to do. AI agents suggest a different relationship: software that can interpret an objective, decide on intermediate steps and take actions across tools.

If that works reliably, the interface changes. The important unit may become an outcome rather than a sequence of clicks. Instead of learning every control, a user describes what needs to happen and supervises the process.

The difficult word there is reliably. Acting creates higher stakes than answering. An incorrect paragraph is inconvenient; an incorrect transaction, deleted file or message sent to the wrong person can be much more serious.

So the interesting design problem is not maximum autonomy. It is useful autonomy with clear boundaries, permissions, verification and recovery.

I think the winners in agentic software may be the products that make delegation feel trustworthy rather than magical. The technology matters, but so does the interface that lets a person remain meaningfully in control.
