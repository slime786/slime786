INVESTING / BUSINESSES

Good businesses make complexity look simple.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · 4 MIN READ

I’m interested in businesses that make a difficult problem feel straightforward to the customer.

That simplicity can hide years of work: distribution, infrastructure, supply chains, software, brand, customer support and disciplined capital allocation. The customer sees a product that works. The business has built a system that makes that experience repeatable.

For me, that is more interesting than trying to predict every short-term movement in a share price. Markets constantly produce noise. Businesses compound through execution.

I like asking questions such as: Why does the customer choose this company? What gets stronger as the company grows? How difficult would it be for a competitor to reproduce the entire system rather than one feature?

This is not a formula for investing. It is simply a useful lens for thinking about quality, durability and why some companies remain relevant for much longer than expected.
