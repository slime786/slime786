AI / INFRASTRUCTURE

Why I keep coming back to AI infrastructure.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · EDITORIAL DRAFT

AI is easiest to notice at the application layer. A new assistant, image generator or coding tool can become visible to millions of people almost overnight. I keep finding myself more interested in what has to exist underneath those experiences.

Every useful AI product ultimately depends on physical and digital infrastructure: chips, data centres, networking, energy, models, storage and increasingly specialised software for inference. The application may feel weightless, but the system behind it is anything but.

That makes infrastructure an interesting way to think about the current cycle. If demand for intelligence keeps increasing, the important question is not only which application wins. It is also where the difficult constraints appear and who is solving them.

I do not assume every constraint lasts forever. Scarcity attracts investment, technology improves and expensive capabilities can become commodities. That is why I want to understand both the bottleneck and what could remove it.

For me, the useful habit is to look one layer beneath the product everyone is discussing. Often that is where the more durable questions begin.
