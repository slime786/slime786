TECHNOLOGY / INFRASTRUCTURE

Infrastructure becomes interesting before it becomes obvious.

MOHEEN MAHMOOD · LONDON · 4 MIN READ

When a new technology wave arrives, attention naturally goes to the applications people can see. But underneath those applications is usually a less visible layer making the entire shift possible.

Cloud computing needed data centres, networking and developer tooling. Mobile needed semiconductors, wireless infrastructure and app distribution. AI is creating its own stack of compute, energy, models, data infrastructure and software tooling.

Infrastructure is interesting because demand can emerge indirectly. A company does not need to build the most famous consumer application to benefit from a platform shift; it may provide something many applications depend on.

The challenge is separating genuine bottlenecks from temporary scarcity. Technology moves quickly, and what looks strategically scarce today can become commoditised tomorrow.

That tension — between essential infrastructure and eventual commoditisation — is one of the areas I want to understand better.
